Comprehensive guide for first-time home buyers in the Austin, TX market, including affordability analysis, down payment options, and step-by-step process guidance.

## Affordability Calculator

### Income-Based Affordability

**Rule of Thumb:** Maximum home price = 3-4x annual household income

| Household Income | Conservative (3x) | Moderate (3.5x) | Aggressive (4x) |
|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| $100,000 | $300,000 | $350,000 | $400,000 |
| $125,000 | $375,000 | $437,500 | $500,000 |
| $150,000 | $450,000 | $525,000 | $600,000 |
| $175,000 | $525,000 | $612,500 | $700,000 |
| $200,000 | $600,000 | $700,000 | $800,000 |

### Debt-to-Income (DTI) Calculation

**Front-End DTI (Housing Only):** Maximum 28% of gross income
**Back-End DTI (All Debt):** Maximum 43% of gross income

**Example: $150,000 Income**
- Monthly gross income: $12,500
- Max housing payment (28%): $3,500
- Max total debt (43%): $5,375

If existing debt is $800/month:
- Available for housing: $5,375 - $800 = $4,575/month

## Down Payment Options

### Conventional Loan - 20% Down
**Pros:**
- No PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)
- Lower monthly payment
- Better interest rates
- Stronger offer to sellers

**Cons:**
- Requires significant savings
- Limits buying power
- Depletes reserves

**Best For:** Buyers with substantial savings who want lowest monthly payment

### Conventional Loan - 10% Down
**Pros:**
- Good balance of buying power and reserves
- PMI is removable at 20% equity
- Competitive interest rates

**Cons:**
- PMI adds ~$200-300/month
- Slightly higher rate than 20% down

**Best For:** Most first-time buyers with good credit

### Conventional Loan - 5% Down
**Pros:**
- Low down payment
- Keeps more cash in reserves
- PMI is removable

**Cons:**
- Higher PMI (~$300-400/month)
- Higher interest rate
- Less equity cushion

**Best For:** Buyers who want to preserve cash

### FHA Loan - 3.5% Down
**Pros:**
- Lowest down payment option
- More flexible credit requirements
- Lower credit score accepted (580+)

**Cons:**
- MIP (Mortgage Insurance Premium) for life of loan
- Higher total cost over time
- Some sellers prefer conventional offers

**Best For:** Buyers with limited savings or lower credit scores

## Texas First-Time Buyer Programs

### Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation (TSAHC)

**My First Texas Home Program**
- Down payment assistance: Up to 5% of loan amount
- Available as grant or forgivable loan
- Income limits apply (varies by county)
- Must be first-time buyer or haven't owned in 3 years

**Eligibility:**
- Credit score: 620+
- Income: Up to 115% of area median income
- Purchase price limits apply

### Austin Housing Finance Corporation (AHFC)

**Down Payment Assistance Program**
- Up to $40,000 in assistance
- 0% interest, deferred payment
- Forgiven after 10 years of occupancy

**Eligibility:**
- Must purchase within Austin city limits
- Income limits: Up to 80% AMI
- First-time buyer or displaced homemaker

### Texas Mortgage Credit Certificate (MCC)

**Tax Credit Program**
- Federal tax credit up to $2,000/year
- Reduces federal income tax liability
- Available for life of the loan

**Eligibility:**
- First-time buyer (or haven't owned in 3 years)
- Income limits apply
- Purchase price limits apply

## The Home Buying Process

### Step 1: Get Pre-Approved (1-2 weeks)
- Gather documents: pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements
- Shop multiple lenders for best rate
- Get pre-approval letter (valid 60-90 days)

### Step 2: Find a Real Estate Agent
- Interview 2-3 agents
- Look for experience in your target areas
- Buyer's agent is typically free (paid by seller)

### Step 3: House Hunting (2-8 weeks)
- Define must-haves vs. nice-to-haves
- Tour homes in person
- Research neighborhoods

### Step 4: Make an Offer
- Work with agent on competitive offer
- Include earnest money (typically 1% of price)
- Negotiate terms and price

### Step 5: Under Contract (30-45 days)
- Home inspection (days 1-10)
- Appraisal (days 10-20)
- Final loan approval (days 20-30)
- Final walkthrough (day before closing)

### Step 6: Closing
- Sign documents
- Pay closing costs
- Get keys!

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

### 1. Not Getting Pre-Approved First
**Problem:** Wasting time looking at homes you can't afford
**Solution:** Get pre-approved before house hunting

### 2. Draining Savings for Down Payment
**Problem:** No reserves for emergencies or repairs
**Solution:** Keep 3-6 months expenses in savings after closing

### 3. Skipping the Home Inspection
**Problem:** Discovering expensive issues after purchase
**Solution:** Always get a professional inspection ($350-500)

### 4. Ignoring Total Monthly Costs
**Problem:** Forgetting taxes, insurance, HOA, maintenance
**Solution:** Budget for total housing cost, not just mortgage

### 5. Making Major Purchases Before Closing
**Problem:** New debt can disqualify your loan
**Solution:** No new credit cards, cars, or large purchases until after closing

## Questions to Ask Your Lender

1. What is the interest rate and APR?
2. What are the total closing costs?
3. Is the rate locked? For how long?
4. What is the PMI cost and when can it be removed?
5. Are there any prepayment penalties?
6. What documents do you need from me?
7. How long will the loan process take?
8. What could delay or derail my loan?
